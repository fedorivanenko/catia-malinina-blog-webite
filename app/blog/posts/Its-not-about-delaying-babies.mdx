What if reproductive aging were optional?<br />
What if fertility weren’t a deadline—but a decision?

If we could extend reproductive health by decades, how would that reshape our lives?

Right now, that’s not reality.<br />
I’m turning 32. That gives me:
 • Three years before fertility decline begins.
 • Eight before it steepens.
 • Thirteen before natural conception becomes unlikely.

The exact timeline differs. The trend does not.<br />
Reproductive aging is brutal—faster and more irreversible than nearly any system in the body.

And so, I face a choice.<br />
A hard, high-stakes, right-now choice.

One path: push forward.<br />
Be a modern renaissance woman. Build brands. Develop biotech.<br />
A strategist. A scientist. A founder.

The other: stop.<br />
Focus on preconception. On pregnancy. On children.<br />
Make space for the future before the door closes.

But these two lives can’t coexist.<br />
Whatever I choose, I lose something.<br />
Time. Opportunity. An alternate version of myself.<br />
And that loss—it’s real.

But what if we didn’t have to choose?

What if we had 20 more fertile years?<br />
Thirty?<br />
What if we lived to 150 <br />— and used that time for deep work, discovery, and family?

I can see it:<br />
Ten years building.<br />
Ten in academia.<br />
Ten launching a biotech startup.<br />
Ten as a VC.<br />
And in between<br />— breaks to have children when it feels right, not when it’s medically urgent.

That would be life.<br />
A full life.<br />
Not “one career + three kids + retire at 65”—but many lives in one body.

And it’s not fantasy.

Lifespan has nearly doubled in a century.<br />
Fertility? Stuck in the past.

But science is moving.<br />
We’re mapping ovarian aging.<br />
Exploring stem cells. Metabolic resets. Cellular rejuvenation.

Reproductive longevity deserves the same status as heart health or cognitive aging.

So why doesn’t it have it?

This isn’t about delaying motherhood.<br />
It’s about expanding human potential.

About giving women the power to commit deeply—without penalty.

We already expect to live to 90 with energy, autonomy, clarity.

Why should fertility be the exception?

Radical life extension is coming.<br />
Will fertility extension come with it?

Because I don’t want to live just one life.<br />
I want to live many.<br />
In the same body.
